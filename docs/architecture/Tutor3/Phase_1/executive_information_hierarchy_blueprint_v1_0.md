# Purpose

This blueprint defines how validated business meaning shall be organized for executive understanding.

Its job is to decide:
- what deserves attention first
- what should support it
- how reading flow should move
- how visual encoding should reduce cognitive load

This document does not build dashboards.
It defines the information order that later presentation layers will implement.

# Executive Information Design Principles

1. Business questions drive hierarchy.
2. Highest-impact information appears first.
3. Visual order must follow business meaning.
4. Cognitive load must be minimized.
5. Support evidence follows primary decision information.
6. No visual recommendation may contradict upstream analytical truth.
7. Future financial content must remain reserved, not fabricated.

---

# Executive Question Stack

The hierarchy should answer these questions in this order:

1. What is the business trying to understand?
2. What decision is the executive trying to make?
3. What information resolves that decision fastest?
4. What supporting evidence is needed to trust the answer?
5. What placeholder space must remain for Alpha’s future financial outputs?

# Information Hierarchy Rules

## Rule 1: Priority
Place the most decision-critical item first.

## Rule 2: Grouping
Group related information together when it reduces effort without hiding meaning.

## Rule 3: Support
Show supporting measures beneath the main decision item.

## Rule 4: Reserve Future Financial Space
Keep reserved structure for Alpha outputs.

## Rule 5: Traceability
Every hierarchy decision must map back to Tutor 1 or Tutor 2 evidence.

## Rule 6: No Silent Correction
If the upstream layers contain contradictions, document them rather than fixing them here.

---

# Proposed Executive Reading Flow

1. Top-line question
2. Core business entity or segment
3. Primary measure or outcome
4. Supporting breakdown
5. Exception, variance, or anomaly
6. Reserved financial section for Alpha

---

# Visual Encoding Recommendations

- Use position for priority
- Use proximity for grouping
- Use size for primary vs supporting information
- Use color sparingly for status or exception emphasis
- Use labels that match business vocabulary
- Avoid decorative elements that do not carry meaning

---

# Validation Checklist

The blueprint is valid only if all are true:

- The business questions are ranked
- The information groups are logically separated
- The reading order is explicit
- The visual strategy is justified
- Cognitive load is reduced
- Tutor 1 and Tutor 2 evidence is preserved
- Alpha placeholders are reserved and not defined
